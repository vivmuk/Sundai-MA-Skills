# Code of Conduct

## Our pledge

We are building shared professional infrastructure for Medical Affairs. Everyone
should be able to contribute regardless of employer, seniority, therapeutic area,
country, background, or identity.

## Expected behaviour

- **Critique the work, not the person.** "This overclaims from single-arm data"
  is useful. "This is amateurish" is not.
- **Bring the evidence.** This is a scientific community. Assertions about what
  is or is not correct practice should cite a guideline, a regulation, or clearly
  flag themselves as one organisation's interpretation.
- **Respect that companies differ.** SOPs vary enormously between organisations
  and jurisdictions. Something being wrong at your company does not make it wrong
  everywhere. That is exactly what `house-rules/` is for.
- **Assume good faith across the competitive boundary.** Contributors work for
  companies that compete with each other. That is fine and expected here.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Harassment, discrimination, or personal attacks.
- Using the project to disparage a competitor's product or a named individual.
- Contributing confidential employer information, patient data, or licensed
  terminology content.
- Attempting to steer the skills toward promotional output, or removing safety
  and compliance guardrails without disclosure.
- Sustained disruption of discussions.

## Competitive and confidentiality boundaries

This is a public repository with contributors from competing organisations. Do
not post confidential commercial information, pricing, unpublished data, or
regulatory correspondence. Do not use issues or PRs to make claims about
competitor products. Where a discussion strays toward commercially sensitive
territory, maintainers will close it without prejudice.

## Reporting

Report conduct concerns by opening a private security advisory (see
`SECURITY.md`) or by contacting the maintainers directly. Reports are handled
confidentially. Maintainers who cannot act impartially — for example on a report
concerning their own employer — will recuse themselves.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may edit, remove, or reject contributions and comments that violate
this code, and may temporarily or permanently ban contributors for behaviour they
consider inappropriate, threatening, or harmful. Enforcement decisions will be
explained where doing so does not compromise a reporter's confidentiality.
