# Patient Engagement Planning

Establish whose experience is needed, what decision their input will change, and
what the organization can honestly act on. Use the synthetic patient partnership
records for workshop work. Do not assume one organization represents all patients.

- Identify missing perspectives, language needs, disability access, caregiver time
  and participation burden. Offer short sessions, breaks, captions and asynchronous
  feedback where useful. Make the activity feasible before requesting participation.
- Separate listening/co-creation, education, research and support services; the
  applicable consent, review and governance route depends on the actual activity.
- Specify what will be collected, how it will be used, compensation arrangements
  where applicable, recording permissions and whether quotations may be attributed.
- Draft neutral questions. Do not seek endorsement, treatment recommendations or
  product uptake. Preserve negative feedback and uncertainty.
- For trial summaries use `plain-language-summary`; for research priorities route
  to `evidence-gap-analysis`. Patient preferences inform priorities but do not
  replace clinical evidence about efficacy or safety.
- Preserve potential safety reports through the appropriate real intake procedure;
  simulated workshop cases remain in the exercise.
- Close the loop with a draft account of what was heard, what will change, what
  cannot change and why. Do not claim a message was sent.

Deliver a participation plan, discussion guide, accessibility provisions, consent
questions for the accountable team and a feedback-to-decision table. Flag input
that cannot be quoted, missing groups and limits on generalization.
Read `house-rules/patient-engagement-planning.md`.
