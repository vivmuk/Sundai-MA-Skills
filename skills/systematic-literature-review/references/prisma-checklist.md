# PRISMA 2020 — Items, and How Each Gets Fudged

The 27-item checklist, with what each item actually requires and the shortcut
that most often gets taken. Source: Page MJ et al. *BMJ* 2021;372:n71.

Use the extension that matches the review: **PRISMA-NMA** (network
meta-analysis), **PRISMA-ScR** (scoping), **PRISMA-S** (search reporting),
**PRISMA-IPD** (individual patient data), **PRISMA-DTA** (diagnostic accuracy).

---

## Title and abstract

**1. Title** — identify the report as a systematic review.
*Fudge:* "A review of…" for something that is not systematic. If you did not
follow a protocol, do not use the word.

**2. Abstract** — the PRISMA 2020 abstract checklist (12 items).
*Fudge:* omitting the number of studies and the certainty of evidence, so the
abstract reads more confidently than the review supports.

## Introduction

**3. Rationale** — what is already known and why another review is needed.

**4. Objectives** — an explicit statement of the question, in PICO terms.
*Fudge:* an objective vague enough to accommodate whatever was found.

## Methods

**5. Eligibility criteria** — inclusion and exclusion, and how studies were
grouped for synthesis.
*Fudge:* criteria that require judgement without stating the rule — "relevant",
"adequate quality", "recent".

**6. Information sources** — every database and register, with the date each was
last searched. Also other sources: websites, organisations, citation searching.
*Fudge:* naming databases without dates, or omitting that only one was searched.

**7. Search strategy** — the **full** strategy for **every** source, including
all filters and limits.
*Fudge:* reproducing the strategy for one database and asserting the others were
"adapted". PRISMA-S exists because this item is so routinely under-reported.

**8. Selection process** — how many reviewers, worked independently or not, and
any automation tools used.
*Fudge:* passive voice concealing that one person screened alone. Single-
reviewer screening is acceptable if declared.

**9. Data collection process** — same disclosures, plus how missing data were
sought from investigators.

**10a. Data items — outcomes** — every outcome sought, all compatible results,
and how you chose when a study reported several measures or time points.
*Fudge:* selecting the time point that suits the narrative without stating a
rule. This is where reviewer-level outcome selection bias enters.

**10b. Data items — other variables** — funding, population characteristics, and
assumptions made about missing information.

**11. Study risk of bias assessment** — the tool, how many assessors, whether
independent.
*Fudge:* "quality was assessed" with no named instrument.

**12. Effect measures** — which measure (RR, OR, HR, mean difference) for each
outcome, specified in advance.

**13a–13f. Synthesis methods** — eligibility for each synthesis; data
preparation; tabulation and visual display; the model and software for
meta-analysis; heterogeneity exploration; sensitivity analyses.
*Fudge:* choosing random effects after seeing the heterogeneity, without saying
so. Pre-specify the model.

**14. Reporting bias assessment** — how risk of missing results was assessed.
*Fudge:* funnel plots with 5 studies, where the test has no power. Say the
assessment was not possible instead.

**15. Certainty assessment** — GRADE or equivalent, per outcome.
*Fudge:* omitted entirely. It is the item most often missing, and the one
guideline committees care most about.

## Results

**16a. Study selection** — the flow diagram, with numbers that reconcile.

**16b. Excluded studies** — studies that looked eligible but were excluded,
**with reasons**.
*Fudge:* a count without the list. This is the most scrutinised item in an HTA
submission, because it is where an inconvenient trial can quietly disappear.

**17. Study characteristics** — cite each included study and present its
characteristics.

**18. Risk of bias in studies** — per study, per outcome, at domain level.
*Fudge:* an overall traffic-light score with no domain detail.

**19. Results of individual studies** — summary statistics and effect estimates
with precision, for every study and outcome.

**20a–20d. Results of syntheses** — characteristics and risk of bias of
contributing studies; the summary estimate with its confidence interval and a
measure of heterogeneity; investigations of heterogeneity; sensitivity analyses.
*Fudge:* reporting the pooled estimate without I², or reporting I² without
interpreting it.

**21. Reporting biases.**

**22. Certainty of evidence** — for each outcome assessed.

## Discussion

**23a. Interpretation** — in the context of other evidence.
*Fudge:* conclusions stated with more confidence than the certainty assessment
in item 22 supports. Cross-check these two against each other; the mismatch is
common and is the single most useful check a reader can run.

**23b. Limitations of the evidence.**
**23c. Limitations of the review process.**
**23d. Implications** for practice, policy, and future research.

## Other information

**24a. Registration and protocol** — registration name and number, or a
statement that the review was not registered.
**24b.** Where the protocol can be accessed.
**24c.** Amendments to the protocol, and why.
*Fudge:* silence about amendments. Amending a protocol is fine; not declaring it
is not.

**25. Support** — funding, and the funder's role.
**26. Competing interests.**
**27. Availability of data, code, and other materials** — extracted data,
analytic code, and any other materials.

---

## The three checks that catch most problems

If you have time to check three things in someone else's review:

1. **Item 16b — the excluded studies list with reasons.** Absent, or present but
   short, is the strongest single signal of a review that is not systematic.
2. **Item 24a against the registered protocol.** Compare the registered primary
   outcome against the reported one. Outcome switching is common and invisible
   unless you look.
3. **Item 22 against item 23a.** Does the discussion claim more than the
   certainty assessment supports? Very often, yes.
