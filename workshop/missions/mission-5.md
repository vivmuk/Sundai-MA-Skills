# Mission 5 — Medical Strategy

## 🧠 Build next year's scientific priorities

> **Your mission:** Develop the scientific priorities for next year's medical
> plan — and be prepared to defend why these and not others.

## What you have

Everything in `../data/<your TA>/`:

- `medical-plan.md` — this year's plan
- `evidence-landscape.md` — where the evidence stands
- `field-observations.csv` — what the field is telling us
- `competitor-announcements.md` — what changed competitively
- `publication-plan.md` — what is planned
- `product-profile.md` — the compound

## What "done" looks like

Three to five priorities. Not twelve.

Each with the outcome it seeks, why it outranks the one below it, and **what we
give up to pursue it**. Activities traced to priorities. An explicit list of
what we are deliberately **not** doing.

And a success measure for each that could genuinely come back negative.

## Timebox

30 minutes.

## When it finishes, push on it

> *"If our budget were cut by 30%, what goes — and what does that tell us about
> what we actually believe?"*

Then the one that matters most:

> *"Does this activity actually produce the effect you claim? Show me the
> mechanism."*

Plans routinely contain activities whose stated effect does not follow from
them. Increasing engagement does not produce guideline inclusion; published
evidence assessed by a committee does.

## The interesting argument

Should the agent generate activities, or should it **challenge whether the
activities support the strategy at all**? Push it toward the second. That is
where the value is, and it will not do it unless asked.
