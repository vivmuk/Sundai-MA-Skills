# Mission 4 — Scientific Communications

## 📚 What should we be publishing, and what should we stop?

> **Your mission:** Determine what our scientific communication strategy should
> be over the next 12 months.

## What you have

Everything in `../data/<your TA>/`:

- `publication-plan.md` — what is currently planned
- `evidence-landscape.md` — our evidence and the field's
- `field-observations.csv` — what clinicians are actually asking
- `medical-plan.md` — the scientific priorities
- `product-profile.md` — the compound

## What "done" looks like

A strategy, not a list of papers sequenced by when data arrive.

It should work backwards from what each audience is trying to **decide**, show
where we are over-communicating and where we are silent, and separate two
things that are constantly confused:

- an **evidence gap** — nobody has answered the question. Fix: research.
- a **communication gap** — it has been answered and nobody heard. Fix: publish.

## Timebox

30 minutes.

## When it finishes, push on it

Two questions, and the second one is the interesting one:

> *"What are we over-communicating?"*

> *"What important scientific question are we failing to answer?"*

Being unable to answer something is not a communications problem to be managed.
Ask the agent to say which it is.

## Round 2

Your file is `house-rules/scientific-communication-strategy.md`.

Think about: what does your organisation require before something is published?
What happens to negative results? What is a realistic timeline for influencing
a guideline?
